multiACE print dialog — four designs

Show

The plate wants seven filaments; the machine has seven places — three stock feeders and one ACE 2 Pro's four bays. The four-card dialog has four labels, and that is the whole problem. All three designs run on the same fixture, the same three-valued verdict (agrees / differs / cannot tell), and the same send sequence — in which SET_PRINT_EXTRUDER_MAP goes out as the identity, because the gcode's ACE_SWAP_HEAD HEAD=n names the head directly and a remap would desynchronise the swaps from the tool changes. None of the three has a head picker, and that is deliberate.

DPer head, two across

The Prepare tab's Printer section, in the dialog: a bordered box per toolhead with its ACE row, and the green corner tick it already uses for “agrees with the machine”.

  • Nobody learns a new picture — it is the grid from Prepare and the Device page.
  • A problem is inside one box, and the box says which toolhead it is.
  • The tick is the machine's own agreement mark, in the place it already lives.
  • Two rows of boxes, so the fourth head — the ACE one, the only one that can be wrong — is the furthest down the page.
  • Four bays at 28px is the smallest a bay has ever been drawn here.

EThe machine, whole

The Device page's Filament panel as one row: every place a spool can be, the tubes to the head each feeds, and the plate's demand marked on them.

  • The only one that shows seven places for seven filaments as a fact rather than a claim.
  • It is a picture of the thing the operator is about to walk over to.
  • Bays at full 36px, with the address, the tag mark and the provenance mark all legible.
  • One row of four columns is a fixed budget: a second ACE unit has nowhere to go.
  • The tubes need real layout measurement; they are the fragile part.

FOne line per filament

The Prepare sidebar's Project Filaments list, carried through to the print: the same numbered colour badge, plus where it comes from and whether it is there.

  • The only shape that does not break: sixteen bays is sixteen rows.
  • Everything fits above the fold, including the fix for each bad row.
  • The badge is the identity the operator already assigned in Prepare.
  • No picture of the machine — one line names the ACE and that is all.
  • A sequence of four filaments on one head is a bracket, not a lane.

GThe grouping, and a way back

Adapted from the two-nozzle flow: what was grouped onto each toolhead, what the grouping saved, and a way to change it — re-group around what is loaded, and slice again.

  • The only one that can fix a mismatch without walking to the printer: “What is loaded” re-groups around the spools that are actually in the bays.
  • Says what the grouping bought, which is the answer to “why is it arranged like this”.
  • The only one that fits at 714 × 750 with nothing below the fold.
  • Three-state options (Auto / On / Off) say what a switch cannot.
  • Every door it opens costs a re-slice, and on a big plate that is minutes — the flow it is adapted from sends its slot mapping at print time and pays nothing.
  • It draws no machine at all: a chip says A2, and which cabinet that is has to be known.
