The three ACE modes

Show

SET_ACE_MODE MODE=normal|multi|head is the printer's own three-way switch, and it decides what a bay address means — so every design that draws one states it. In head mode a toolhead is its own feeder or wired to exactly one unit, and A2 is the second bay of that cabinet. In multi there is no such wiring: bay i of every unit is plumbed to head i, so a head has a lane and its places are one bay out of each cabinet. Toolhead 1 can be fed A1 or B1 and nothing else — A2 is unreachable from it at any price.

These follow the Device page, which is the surface built against the live mode switch. Worth knowing: the Prepare tab disagrees. Plater.cpp:9845 labels this mode “Units pooled onto a single ACE head” and PrintConfig.cpp:3995 “Combined pools several units onto a single head” — one says every head gets a lane, the other says every unit converges on one head. Only one can be true of the hardware, and it has not been settled here.

GThe grouping, and a way back

The badge sits beside Toolhead N — once per head, because which unit feeds a head is a property of the head. In multi there is no unit to name, so the head carries its lane instead and the cabinets move to a band.

  • One badge per head, not one per chip: the first draft drew the same cabinet three times on one toolhead.
  • The band above the grid is the Device page's own placement for multi — nothing about a cabinet belongs on a head that is not wired to one.
  • A lane is four words where head mode is a picture.

DPer head, two across

Each box draws the hardware behind its head — one cabinet's four bays in head mode, one bay from each cabinet in multi.

  • The lane is drawn: two one-bay cabinets side by side is what the plumbing actually is.
  • Two boxes of two bays looks like less machine than it is.

EThe machine, whole

Every place and every tube in one row, so a lane is visibly a bay from each cabinet converging on one head.

  • The only one where the splitter is legible — two cabinets, one head.
  • Eight cabinets' worth of drawing in a 686 px card.
