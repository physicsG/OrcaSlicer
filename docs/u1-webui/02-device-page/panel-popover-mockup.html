02What changed, and why

  1. All four toolheads, icon-only

    Nozzle glyph with a number badge, a filament colour chip, then 27 / 0 °C. No “Toolhead 1” label anywhere.

    Why: the label was the least informative thing in the row and it cost the width that the reading needs. The badge carries the identity, and slot 4 wears the accent because the machine reports it live. Slot 3 gets a hatched chip rather than an empty circle — “no filament” and “white filament” must not look alike.
  2. Targets edit in place

    The target is an <input type="number"> that looks like text until you touch it: hover paints a soft field, focus outlines the whole row, and min/max turn the value amber outside the limits.

    The cursor you asked about is the I-beam — CSS cursor: text. The blinking mark it leaves behind is the caret. Both come free with a real input, which is also why this is an input and not a styled span: it brings keyboard, selection and validation with it.
  3. The ring is the step size

    Outer ring 10 mm, inner ring 1 mm, quadrant chooses the axis, centre homes.

    Why: this is the good idea in the Bambu wheel. My previous pad had a separate 10/1/0.1 selector, which meant every move was two clicks and carried hidden state — you could not tell what a press would do without reading another control first. Here the target you click is the distance.
  4. Z is a row, not a third axis on the wheel

    ↑10 ↑1 · Bed · ↓1 ↓10 under the wheel.

    Why: on this machine Z moves the bed, not the head. Putting it on the same wheel would imply the toolhead travels, and the label “Bed” between the arrows says which way the object actually goes.
  5. Extruder gets its own column

    A 1–4 selector above ▲/▼, captioned Extruder.

    Why: extrusion is per-toolhead and unbounded, so it does not belong on a wheel whose whole meaning is bounded distance. The selector here chooses which head extrudes — it does not change the live tool; that stays a deliberate action with its blocking modal.
  6. Quick settings became tiles

    Speed, cooling, purifier and lamp sit as a row of tiles under the readings, each opening its panel directly beneath.

    Why: it matches the reference, and it separates readings you watch from settings you change. The lamp is a binary, so it stays a switch with no panel.

03Open questions

Does the 0.1 mm step survive? Two rings give 10 and 1. A third ring is possible but the innermost band gets thin and the home target shrinks. Alternative: hold a modifier, or drop 0.1 as too fine to be useful from a jog pad.
The wheel needs an unhomed state. Klipper refuses a move on an unhomed axis, and this machine currently reports homed_axes: "". The sectors should read as disabled until G28 has run, with the centre inviting it — otherwise every press silently does nothing, which is the bug that started this thread.
