Direct selection, anchored under the control

A design study for the U1 Device tab’s Control panel — replacing centred modal sheets with panels that open beneath the thing you clicked.

01The pattern, live

Click any row. The row keeps its place and takes a highlight; its options open directly underneath. One panel at a time, Escape or a click outside closes it.

Control
jog pad, extrude, home — unchanged

02Why this beats the modal sheet

A centred modal answers “what do you want to set?” by hiding the thing you were looking at. Every value in this column is a glanceable reading first and a setting second — the bed is 25 °C, the fan is 85%. Covering the panel to change one of them throws away the context that made you want to change it.
It is not free. An anchored popover has to solve placement (it will overflow a 172 px column), focus, and dismissal. Those are handled once in overlay.js and then every row gets them — which is the argument for doing it properly rather than per-row.

03Design pointers

  1. The trigger stays visible and owns the panel

    The clicked row keeps its position and takes a 2 px accent border plus a tinted fill. Nothing else moves.

    Why: the highlight is what makes the floating panel legible as “belonging to this row” rather than to the page. Without it a popover is just a box that appeared.
  2. Name the stops, don’t just number them

    Silent / Standard / Sport reads faster than 50 / 100 / 150, so the number becomes the subtitle rather than the label.

    Why: it is what the Bambu control gets right. People choose an intent, not a percentage — and the U1 accepts exactly three speeds, so a continuous slider was always over-promising.
  3. Keep the current value on the row face

    Every row shows its reading without being opened, and the reading turns accent when its panel is open.

    Why: the panel is for changing, not for finding out. If you have to open it to read the value, the column has failed at its main job.
  4. Anchor to the row, size to the content

    The popover is left-aligned to its row and free to be wider than the 172 px column, with a small caret pointing back at its origin.

    Why: constraining it to the column width would force two-line labels and a scrollbar on a four-stop control.
  5. Not everything earns a panel

    A binary stays an inline switch. A mechanical, slow, interrupting action keeps its blocking modal.

    Why: a popover for an on/off toggle adds a click and a dismissal to a decision that had neither. And a toolchange should take the screen — the gantry is moving and a stray second command is a real hazard.
  6. Say what a setting will and won’t do

    One muted line at the top of the panel, as Bambu’s “This only takes effect during printing”.

    Why: cheapest possible way to stop a support question. The speed factor on an idle printer genuinely does nothing.
  7. Apply on click, not on OK

    Picking a stop sends it. There is no confirm button on a reversible setting.

    Why: a Cancel/OK pair implies risk. Fan speed has none — and the row’s own value is the confirmation.

05Two layouts, and a recommendation

The Bambu control is a horizontal strip of tiles. The U1’s column is vertical, 172 px, and its geometry was measured against the shipped page.

Recommended: keep the vertical column, add the highlight and the anchored popover (what is drawn above). It changes interaction without touching a layout that was matched panel-header-to-panel-header with the original.
Rejected: a horizontal tile strip. Closer to the screenshot, but it would re-flow the Control panel and break the 2×2 grid breakpoint the reconstruction was measured into. The interaction is the part worth copying; the axis is not.
