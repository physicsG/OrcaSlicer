02Why this beats the modal sheet

A centred modal answers “what do you want to set?” by hiding the thing you were looking at. Every value in this column is a glanceable reading first and a setting second — the bed is 25 °C, the active toolhead is 26 °C. Covering the panel to change one of them throws away the context that made you want to change it.
It is not free. An anchored popover has to solve placement (it overflows a 176 px column by design), focus, and dismissal. Those get solved once in overlay.js and every row inherits them — which is the argument for building it as one primitive rather than per row.

03Design pointers

  1. The trigger stays visible and owns the panel

    The clicked row keeps its position and takes a 2 px accent border with a tinted fill. Nothing else moves.

    Why: the highlight is what makes a floating panel read as “belonging to this row” rather than to the page. Without it a popover is just a box that appeared.
  2. Name the stops, don’t just number them

    Silent / Standard / Sport reads faster than 50 / 100 / 150, so the number becomes the subtitle.

    Why: it is what your reference gets right — people choose an intent, not a percentage. And the U1 accepts exactly three speeds, so the continuous slider was over-promising all along.
  3. Keep the reading on the row face

    Every row shows its value closed, and that value turns accent when its panel opens.

    Why: the panel is for changing, not for finding out. If you must open it to read the value, the column has failed at its main job.
  4. Anchor to the row, size to the content

    Left-aligned to its row, wider than the column, with a caret pointing back at its origin.

    Why: constraining it to 176 px would force two-line labels and a scrollbar onto a four-stop control.
  5. Not everything earns a panel

    The chamber light stays an inline switch. Pick extruder keeps its blocking modal.

    Why: a popover for an on/off toggle adds a click and a dismissal to a decision that had neither. And a toolchange should take the screen — the gantry is moving, and a stray second command is a real hazard.
  6. Say what a setting will and won’t do

    One muted line at the top, as with “This only takes effect during printing”.

    Why: the cheapest possible way to prevent a support question. The speed factor on an idle printer genuinely does nothing.
  7. Apply on click, not on OK

    Picking a stop sends it. Only the free-text field keeps a Set button.

    Why: a Cancel/OK pair implies risk. Fan speed has none, and the row’s own value is the confirmation. A typed number is different — it is not chosen from a safe set.

05Two layouts, and a recommendation

Recommended: keep the vertical column. Add the highlight and the anchored popover — what is drawn above. It changes the interaction without touching a layout whose geometry was measured panel-header-to-panel-header against the shipped page.
Considered and rejected: a horizontal tile strip. Closer to the screenshot, but it would re-flow the Control panel and break the 2×2 grid breakpoint the reconstruction was measured into. The interaction is the part worth copying; the axis is not. Say the word and I will draw that variant too.
